Privacy Policy

Shrink Image&Video · Android app · package com.sunsinedimon.imageoptimayzer

Effective date: 30 September 2026

The short version. This app is completely offline. It does not collect, transmit, sell or share any personal data, because it has no way to send anything anywhere: it does not even request Android's internet permission. There is no account, no sign-in, no advertising, and no analytics. Your photos, videos and their location tags are processed on your own device and stay there.

The one exception: if you email us, we receive that email and use it to reply. See section 16.

2. We collect no personal data

The developer does not collect, receive, transmit, sell, rent or otherwise share any personal data, and there is no server to receive it. Specifically:

  • No analytics or telemetry. No usage statistics, no crash reports, no diagnostic uploads, no A/B tests, no event logging.
  • No advertising. The app contains no ads and no advertising or tracking identifiers. It does not read the Android advertising ID.
  • No account. You cannot create an account or sign in, so there is no user ID, no name, no email address, and no password to store.
  • No contacts, camera, microphone, SMS, call log, Bluetooth, or device identifier. None of these permissions are requested.
  • No data sold or shared. Not with advertisers, not with data brokers, not with analytics providers, not with anyone else.

Because no personal data is collected off your device, the Google Play Data safety section for this app declares that no data is collected and no data is shared.

3. No internet access

The app does not declare the android.permission.INTERNET permission in its manifest. Android does not allow it to open a network connection at all. It contains no HTTP client, no web view, and no analytics or advertising SDK. This is a property of the published binary, not a setting you have to trust: the capability is absent from the app.

The only external software that can be reached is Google's own Google Play infrastructure, and only if you choose to use it. See section 12.

4. What the app does with your files

The app resizes, recompresses and optionally converts the photos and videos you select. This is done entirely on your device using the phone's own image and video codecs. Specifically:

  • Your files are read from the location you choose, processed in memory or in the app's private cache, and written back to the same location — or to an output folder you choose instead.
  • Your original file is replaced only if the new file is genuinely smaller and all of the file's metadata was preserved and verified. If anything cannot be preserved, the file is skipped and your original is left untouched.
  • Your image and video files are never transmitted, uploaded, or used for any purpose other than the optimisation you asked for.

5. Folder access uses the system file picker

The app does not ask for blanket access to your photos, and it does not request READ_MEDIA_IMAGES, READ_MEDIA_VIDEO or READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE. Instead, it uses Android's standard Storage Access Framework: you open the system folder picker, choose a folder or specific files, and the system grants the app access to exactly that and nothing else.

That grant is stored by the operating system, not sent to us. The app remembers the folder you picked so it can offer it next time, and re-checks on every launch that the permission is still valid; if you revoke access, the app forgets it immediately. You can revoke access at any time from Android Settings → Apps → Shrink Image&Video → Storage and cache → Clear access, or simply by picking a different folder next run.

6. Location metadata in your photos

This is the one piece of sensitive information the app touches, and it does so for a specific reason.

Modern Android versions hide GPS coordinates from apps by default when they read a media file, as part of the location-privacy protections introduced for photo pickers. For a metadata-preserving compressor, that default is the single biggest risk: a photo that is silently stripped of its location is a photo the user did not agree to lose. The app therefore optionally requests the ACCESS_MEDIA_LOCATION permission, so it can read the original, unredacted GPS tags and copy them into the optimised file.

In practice, that means:

  • GPS coordinates are read from your file's EXIF/XMP tags and written back into the optimised file. They are preserved, not harvested.
  • Coordinates are never written to the app's history, its logs, the clipboard, or anywhere else.
  • Coordinates are never transmitted anywhere, and are not used to determine your location, track you, or build a profile.
  • If you decline the permission, the app still works. Files whose location tags the system has hidden are processed without them, and the app tells you this happened for that file rather than silently writing a version with tags missing.

The app does not use the device's GPS, network, or cell location at all. It never asks where you are; it only reads coordinates that were already inside the pictures you selected.

8. What the app stores on your device

The app does store some information locally, in Android's private app storage. This storage is sandboxed: other apps cannot read it, and it is deleted when you uninstall the app. Nothing in it is ever transmitted.

Run history (your optimisation log)

So you can review what happened in a batch, the app keeps a local record in a private database (imageoptimayzer.db):

  • the folder name you selected, and the date of the run;
  • for each file: its name, its storage identifier, its format, its size before and after, its pixel dimensions, and whether it was optimised, skipped or failed;
  • why a file was skipped, when a file was too small to improve on.

This record contains file names and local storage identifiers. It does not contain your photos, their contents, or any GPS coordinates.

Settings

Your chosen resize mode, output format, quality values, the folder you last selected, and a running total of how much space the app has saved. Stored locally, on the device only.

Temporary files

While processing, the app writes scratch files (for example an in-progress video or a metadata splice) into the app's cache directory. These are deleted when the run finishes, and Android may clear the cache at any time.

Diagnostic log

The app keeps a short technical log of the most recent run, so you can see exactly why a file was skipped. It is held in memory only, is never written to disk, and disappears when the app process ends. You can also clear it at any time with Settings → Diagnostics → Clear logs, or copy it to your clipboard yourself if you want to share it. Nothing in it contains GPS coordinates.

9. Your data is not uploaded to cloud backup

Some Android apps let the operating system copy their private files to your Google Drive backup. This app explicitly opts out: the run history and settings are excluded from Android's automatic cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer. The app's local data does not travel to Google's servers, and there is no developer-operated backup, because there is no developer-operated anything.

A consequence: if you move to a new phone, your settings and run history are not carried over. You will set the app up again on the new device. This is a deliberate trade-off in favour of nothing leaving your device.

10. How to delete your data

Everything the app stores is on your device, so you are always in control of it:

  • Delete the run history: open the History screen and use Clear history. This permanently erases every run and file record listed in section 8. Your photos and videos are not touched.
  • Delete the diagnostic log: Settings → Diagnostics → Clear logs.
  • Delete everything: Android Settings → Apps → Shrink Image&Video → Storage & cache → Clear storage, or simply uninstall the app. This removes the database, the settings and all cache files.
  • Revoke folder access: Android Settings → Apps → Shrink Image&Video → Storage and cache → Clear access.

The app has no server-side copy of anything, so there is nothing further for us to delete on request. If you would like us to confirm deletion, contact us using section 16.

11. No third-party tracking libraries

The app bundles no analytics, advertising, attribution, crash-reporting, fingerprinting or social SDK. It does not include Google Play Services. The open-source libraries it does bundle — AndroidX, Jetpack Compose, Hilt, Room, DataStore, WorkManager, AndroidX ExifInterface, Google metadata-extractor, AndroidX Media3, and the Google Play in-app review and billing libraries — are used only for the features described above and perform no tracking. Code shrinking and obfuscation is enabled in release builds.

12. Google Play purchases and ratings (optional, your choice)

Two optional features involve Google Play rather than the developer. You can use the app fully without ever touching either.

Ratings and reviews

If you choose to rate the app, Android's in-app review dialog opens. The rating is sent to Google and is published on your Play Store listing. The developer neither receives nor sees individual ratings or the identity of the person who left one. Manage or delete your review in Google Play → your profile → Reviews.

Voluntary "support the developer" purchase

If you choose to make the optional in-app purchase, the transaction is completed entirely by Google Play Billing. The developer receives an aggregate statement and a purchase token; the app does not receive or store your name, card details, billing address, or full payment information, and cannot see them. Refunds are handled by Google under the Google Play refund policy.

Purchases made through Google Play are also subject to Google's Privacy Policy and the Google Play Terms of Service, which govern that data, not this one. If you never make a purchase, the app transmits nothing to Google other than as described above.
